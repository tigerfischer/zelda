# Generic Call Brief — Zelva Sales Call with Dental Clinic Owner

**Format:** 10–12 minutes max. Any longer and you lose them.  
**Mindset:** You are a growth advisor who happens to know dental specifically — not a salesperson running a pitch deck.  
**Goal of this call:** A clear "yes" to a 2-week free trial, OR a clear "no" so you move on. Not a follow-up loop.

---

## Before the Call

Know these three things about the clinic before you dial:
1. How many Google reviews they have and what their rating is
2. Whether their Google Business Profile has a description (or is incomplete)
3. Whether they have a website / online booking

This takes 60 seconds on Google Maps. Without it, you're guessing at their pain.

---

## Call Structure

### 1. Opening — 30 seconds

Establish who you are and why this isn't a cold call.

> "Hi Dr. [Name], this is [Your Name] from Zelva — I sent you a WhatsApp message a couple of days ago about your clinic. Thanks for picking up. I promise I'll keep this short — do you have about 10 minutes?"

Wait for a yes before continuing. If they say "make it quick" — respect that, cut to discovery.

**What to avoid:** Don't say "I'm calling to tell you about our platform." You'll lose them in 15 seconds.

---

### 2. Discovery — 3 minutes

Ask before pitching. You're trying to understand which of the three pains is sharpest for them. Let them talk.

**Opening question (always ask this first):**
> "Quick question — right now, how are most of your new patients finding you? Is it mostly word-of-mouth, or are you getting people from Google or Practo as well?"

**Follow-up questions — pick 2 based on what they said:**

| If they said... | Ask... |
|---|---|
| Mostly word-of-mouth / referrals | "Does that feel stable, or do you have months where it's slower than you'd like?" |
| We get some from Google | "How many new patients a month would you say come from Google specifically? Is that tracking up or flat?" |
| We're on Practo | "How's that working for you? Are the leads actually converting, or is it mostly tyre-kickers?" |
| We get a lot of no-shows | "What's your current process when someone doesn't show up — do you follow up, or does that appointment just go?" |
| We're pretty busy | "That's good to hear. Are you doing anything to bring back patients who haven't visited in 6 months or more?" |

**Always end discovery with:**
> "And do you have any kind of system today for asking your happy patients to leave a Google review?"

Almost universally the answer is no. This is your bridge.

---

### 3. Bridge — 30 seconds

Reflect what you heard back to them. This shows you listened and makes the pitch feel tailored, not scripted.

> "Okay, so what I'm hearing is — most of your growth is still referral-dependent, you're not systematically getting Google reviews from your patients, and you don't have a way to bring back patients who've gone quiet. Does that sound right?"

Wait for them to confirm. If they correct you, adjust. This moment of "yes, that's exactly it" is what makes the next 90 seconds land.

---

### 4. The Pitch — 90 seconds

Three capabilities, in this order, tied to what they just said.

> "So that's exactly what Zelva is built to fix — and specifically for dental clinics, not a generic tool."

**GBP optimization → more walk-ins and calls:**
> "First, we optimize your Google Business Profile — your description, your categories, how your reviews are structured — so when someone searches 'dentist in [City],' your clinic shows up higher and your profile actually converts that search into a call."

**Automated review collection:**
> "Second, after every appointment, your patient automatically gets a WhatsApp from your clinic asking them to leave a Google review. You don't do anything — it goes out, they tap the link, they leave a review. Most clinics go from 2–3 reviews a month to 15–20."

**Appointment reminders:**
> "Third, every patient gets a WhatsApp reminder 24 hours before their appointment to confirm. The ones who can't make it reschedule instead of just not showing up. No-shows drop meaningfully."

**Patient recall:**
> "And for patients who haven't visited in 6 months, we send them a gentle WhatsApp from your clinic — 'Hi [Patient Name], it's been a while, time for a check-up?' — and they book. Completely automated."

**Then stop.** Don't add more. Ask:
> "Does any of that map to what you were just describing?"

---

### 5. Objection Handling

**"I'm already on Practo / JustDial."**
> "That's great for top-of-funnel — but Practo owns that patient relationship, not you. The patient who came through Practo sees your competitors on the same screen. What we do is help you move that patient into a direct relationship with your clinic — your WhatsApp, your Google reviews, your recall. That's yours permanently, regardless of what Practo changes their pricing to."

**"I don't have time to manage another tool."**
> "That's actually why we built it the way we did. After a one-time setup — which we handle — there's nothing for you to do. It runs automatically. The only thing you'll see is more Google reviews coming in and patients confirming their appointments."

**"How much does it cost?"**
> "Let's not worry about that just yet — I'd rather you see it working for your clinic first. What I'd like to do is set up a 2-week trial at no cost, so you can see the actual numbers for your practice. If it's not working for you, you walk away with no obligation. Fair?"

(If they push: "We're at ₹3,000–5,000 a month depending on the plan — less than the revenue from one filling.")

**"I already have a marketing agency."**
> "Good — we work alongside that, not instead of it. What we do is very specific: Google reviews, GBP, WhatsApp reminders, patient recall. Most agencies don't touch any of that. What results are you seeing from them currently on the Google review side?"

**"Send me more information."**
> "Absolutely — I'll send you a quick 2-minute video over WhatsApp that shows exactly how it works for a clinic similar to yours. But before I do — if that video answered all your questions, would you be open to trying it for 2 weeks?"

(Get a soft yes before sending anything. Otherwise the material goes into a black hole.)

**"I'll think about it / call me next week."**
> "Of course — what's the main thing you'd want to think through? Is it the time involved, the cost, or something else?"

Listen to what they say. The real objection is usually one of three: trust (will this work?), time (do I have to manage it?), or money (can I justify it?). Address that specifically.

---

### 6. Close — 60 seconds

Every call should end with one of three outcomes:

**Best case — trial close:**
> "Here's what I'd suggest: let me set up the 2-week trial for [Clinic Name]. I'll need about 15 minutes with you or your receptionist to do the initial setup — Google Business Profile access, the WhatsApp number you want to use. We can do that over a video call or I can walk you through it on WhatsApp. Which works better for you?"

**Second best — video follow-up with a commitment:**
> "Let me send you the 2-minute demo video right now over WhatsApp. If it makes sense, we can do the setup call tomorrow. What time are you usually free for 15 minutes?"

**Graceful exit — if they're genuinely not interested:**
> "Totally understand. Can I ask — is it that this isn't a priority right now, or is there something specific about Zelva that didn't land? I want to make sure I'm not wasting your time if I follow up in a few months."

This tells you whether to recycle them later.

---

## What NOT to Do

- **Don't pitch before you discover.** If you start with features before asking one question, you'll sound like every other salesperson.
- **Don't read a script.** Use this brief to internalise the structure, then have a real conversation.
- **Don't overpromise.** "Guaranteed 50% more patients" is something you can't back up and destroys trust.
- **Don't mention price first.** It anchors the conversation in cost before value.
- **Don't send material without a next step.** If they ask for a brochure, agree on what happens after they read it.
- **Don't call after 7pm or before 9am.** Doctors are in practice during the day — best windows are 1–3pm (post-lunch lull) or after 6:30pm.

---

## Signals That This Lead Is Hot

- They answered the discovery questions with specific numbers ("we get about 5–8 new patients a month from Google")
- They mentioned no-shows unprompted
- They've tried Practo and are frustrated
- They asked about price on their own (buying intent, not resistance)
- They said "that's exactly what I need" at any point

If you hit two or more of these, push harder on the trial close immediately — don't wait for the end of the call.

---

## After the Call

Within 5 minutes of hanging up, send a WhatsApp:

> "Dr. [Name], great speaking with you. [One line that references something specific they said.] Here's the demo video: [link]. Let me know when you're free for the 15-minute setup call — I can do [Day] at [Time] or [Day] at [Time]."

Personalise the middle sentence. "Great speaking" followed by a generic link is forgettable.
